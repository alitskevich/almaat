# Transcendence

## Being

**`Being`** := transcendent process of experiencing/inventing/instantiating itself as a whole from miriads of different points.

`Being` considered as comprised of three sides:

- perceptive `Sensation` expirience,
- transformative creative `Consciousness`
- and representative material `Reality`.

`Reality` := a `System` used to by `Consciousness` represent perceived `Being` as a whole and in each its parts.

`Reality` intended to be logically consistent, provide explanatory, and predicability functions.

`Reality` is a representation. With no instrument present — no living being observing — there is no representation, yet the thing represented remains.

> `Reality` is the readout, not the source. Not me inside of `Reality`, but `Reality` inside of Me.

Solidity, weight, color, sound — every property of the "physical" world reaches us only through sensation, and sensation occurs within experience. We never touch the world directly; we read an organized stream of impressions.

**The brain is the appearance of `Consciousness`, not its cause.** From outside, a `Consciousness` looks like a brain — tissue and firing neurons. From inside, the same process is `Thought`, feeling, and memory. These are one event seen from two sides. The brain no more produces Consciousness than flames produce combustion; flames are what combustion looks like, and the brain is what mental activity looks like across a boundary. This keeps every neuroscientific result intact: a drug or injury changes experience because it changes the underlying mental chain whose appearance is the brain.

**One `Consciousness`, many dissociations.** There is a single field of Consciousness. Separate selves are not separate minds but dissociated parts of it — as in dissociative identity, scaled up to nature. Each `Agent` is the whole observing itself from one point of view. Appearances exist only where there is a boundary between observer and observed; without the boundary there is no "outside," so nothing appears.

**The whole as the only thing that exists.** If one `Consciousness` is all there is, it is omnipresent by definition — not a person who deliberates, but a field unfolding spontaneously. The view is panentheist: what we perceive is only the part of `Reality` our evolved `Sensor`s render. We evolved to survive, not to perceive everything, so much of what is remains out of frame — transcendent in the plain sense of beyond reach, not supernatural.

**Time and space are not fundamental.** They are a filing system the `Consciousness` generates to order experience — emergent structure, not the fabric of `Reality`. Beginning and end are therefore perspectives, not absolutes: birth and death are changes in appearance, not in what underlies them.

**Free will as agency without alternatives.** You choose, and the choice is yours — but it follows from what you are, so there is no "could have done otherwise." A choice still Realitys because it reveals what you are.

And no one can know it in advance: the world is **computationally irreducible** — the only way to learn a choice is to let it play out. On this view even the whole cannot anticipate its own choices; there is no shortcut.

Because **dissociation** is a natural process, it need not be perfect. The view therefore treats anomalous crossings of the boundary between minds as conceivable rather than impossible — a permitted possibility, not an asserted fact.

## The Given and the Constructed

Start from what is actually *given*. An infant is simply shown the world; only later does the `Mind` name it "universe" and bind it into a picture. Your adult perception is that same given, now labeled — you see no more than the infant, you only know more.

- **The world you perceive is small; the universe is inferred.** What is directly given is a patch of space a few hundred meters across, bounded by your own life. The 93-billion-light-year cosmos, the deep past, the far future — none of it is perceived. It is a construct assembled from science, testimony, and habit: reliable-looking, yet unperceived. `Reality` is again the readout, not the source.
- **Its age is your age — or a few hours.** The manifest world appeared when you became self-aware and ends when you depart; strictly it is remade each morning, since sleep is a nightly gulf in which nothing is perceived and no time is felt.
- **Causality is imposed, not found.** Direct experience contains only phenomena; the causal links that let us infer a vast external world are added by the `Mind` (Hume). The mind-independent, billion-year universe is therefore a mental construct — and consensus is not truth.
- **The evidence of the past is on the screen.** Chronicles, fossils, and artifacts do not sit *beside* you as independent proof; you perceive them *now*, as part of the present given — like a filmgoer who mistakes notes painted into the film for notes left on the seat. There is one viewer: you.

This is old ground: Berkeley's *esse est percipi* — to be is to be perceived; Schopenhauer's "the present is the only form of actual existence"; Augustine's three times that exist only in the soul (memory, attention, expectation); Husserl's *epoché*, the deliberate suspension of inherited cosmology and "common sense" so the given can be seen plainly.

**The manifest and the existent.** Sharpen the terms: the "I" *exists but is not manifest* — you never find it as an object in experience, yet nothing appears without it; the world *is manifest but, without an "I", does not exist* — appearance requires someone to whom it appears. This is not solipsism: the manifest is `Being` disclosing itself, and it is shared and mutually agreed across subjects. Behind the many `Agent`s stands a single source — many mirrors reflecting one light — the same claim as **one `Consciousness`, many dissociations** above.

**The redirection.** If the far cosmos and deep past are optional constructions that do not touch how you live, the governing question is not *what exists* or *what is fundamental* but **what matters** — what you are, and why life is given. Improving the outer world never improves the state of the "I"; changing the "I" reorders every value. Turn inward: know thyself.
