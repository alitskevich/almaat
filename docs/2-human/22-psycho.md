# The Living Animal

**Animal**: The interwine of psychic, energetic, and hormonal processes
that aimed to satisfy animalistic needs:

- individual survival, safety, health, satiety, peace
- and species continuation and Care for offspring.

Animal:

- is driven by emotions, instincts, and impulses.
- is reactive, not proactive — responding to stimuli rather than initiating action.
- is focused on the present moment, with limited capacity for long-term planning or abstract thinking.
- is easily hijacked by external influences (media, social norms, marketing) that exploit its vulnerabilities for profit or control.
- is prone to cognitive biases and irrational behaviors that undermine its well-being and growth.
- is the source of both our greatest joys and deepest sufferings — but also the key to unlocking our full potential as human beings.

Thus:

- avoids suffering, energy expenditure, and uncertainty.
- prefers simple over complex, familiar over unknown, quick results over delayed, predictability over success.
- seeks pleasure, comfort, and social connection.

**Emotion**: Organism arousal in response to stimuli perceived as a threat to the Animal.

- Signals the presence of a threat and mobilizes energy for action.
- Can be triggered by real or imagined threats, and can persist even after the threat is gone.
- Can be positive (joy, love) or negative (fear, anger), but all emotions are ultimately rooted in the same underlying physiological processes.
- Can be regulated through various techniques (breathing, mindfulness, cognitive reframing) to reduce their intensity and duration, and to promote more adaptive responses to stressors.

- Intensity depends on: novelty, precision, stability, vividness of the stimulus, and importance of the corresponding perception
- Manifests in: heart rate, blood pressure, eye movement, pupil dilation, facial expression, gesture

**Stress**: Organism mobilization for decision-making in response to sudden danger.

- Shuts down rational thinking; suppresses immune system
- Triggers one of three responses: fight, flee, or adapt

The Animal constantly scans for threats — and invents them when none exist.

**Fear**: Disconnection from reasoning caused by insufficient grounds for decision in a stressful situation.

**Rage**: Release of excess energy after hyper-mobilization,
following a decision made by the Animal in a stressful situation.

**Laughter**: Reaction to perception shift that caused a pattern break —
originally the relief of discovering no real threat in what caused fear.

Humor reveals the unexpected structure of reality and helps us cope with it.

**Feeling**: A lasting emotional connection with a person, situation, or object

## Persona

**Persona**: manifestation of a Human in its communication/relations with other People.

Persona is built around its **Identity**: a continuous story about oneself,
where boundaries, self-esteem, guidances and imperatives to follow
are formed and maintained.

Human identity is a multi-layered construct integrating:

- biology (what we are),
- mind (who we feel we are),
- society (our roles),
- culture (shared meaning),
- and personal narrative (our life story).

**The Three Sides of Identity**:

1. **PerceivedSelf** — who I perceive myself to be
2. **AspirationalSelf (Hero)** — who I want to become
3. **ProjectedSelf (Mask)** — who I want others to perceive me as

> The gap between these sides is psychically painful,
but is also the source of emotional drive toward development/growth.

Persona is seeking for social connection over personal comfort:

- Belonging: acceptance and feeling of being part to the own tribe - *I am like you, I am with you all!*
- Validation: external approval, recognition and respect of Identity from others - *I am valuable, I am worthy of love and respect!*
- Significance: powerful status, enticed value, admiration, worship - *I am important, I am special, I am better than others!*
- and thus, multiplicative social support, protection, and resources. - *I am safe, I am comfortable, I am happy!*

### Social Mask

**Social Mask**: Stable set of strategies to use in interaction with social Environment —
aimed to gaining more external Validation and support from community.

**Functions**:

- Hide weak/vulnerable sides — shame, guilt, loneliness, pain, fear
- Show attractive sides that evoke approval and respect from Environment
- Soften possible stresses and reduce their risks

**Reactions**:

- In response to contempt or rejection: becomes hurt, turns angry and vengeful — toward self and everyone around
- In response to deterioration/loss of social status: demands to be like everyone, not worse than others, divides into "us" and "them"

**The Marketing Character (Erich Fromm)**: *"There exists a type of people whose entire life is directed toward receiving recognition and admiration. Fromm described a character oriented toward sales, toward displaying one's qualities and, more broadly, oneself. As a rule, such people have a deep inner conviction that they are not valuable in themselves and cannot be loved just like that. To prove their worth and right to love, they must do something, always be first, and receive admiration necessary like air. But even when they manage to receive love and recognition, they do not calm down: because now they love not them, but their mask, their successes and achievements."*  

### Dark Shadow

**Dark Shadow**: Hidden 'dark' subconscious side of the Persona — suppressed during upbringing, forbidden by external social frameworks, hidden under the opposing external Mask.  

**Properties**:

- Persona needs can suppress Animal ones and push them into the subconscious space
- A strongly assigned social Mask of Masculinity leads to formation of the Anima, and vice versa
- Mask and Shadow are products of upbringing through experiencing pain and seeking reward in close environment in childhood

**Charisma**: The distinctive uniqueness and attractiveness of an integrated person.

The Whole Person arises through conscious recognition and acceptance of one's shadow, making peace with it.

- It is the source of authenticity, creativity, and emotional depth.
- It evokes interest, respect, and inspiration in others — but also fear.

## The Predictive Brain

Your brain is not reactive, but a prediction engine constructing reality from past experience, updated by sensory data.

It feels like "seeing" is controlled hallucination. This is why you can catch a ball — a purely reactive brain would be too slow.

*Implication*: Much of what feels like objective reality is your brain's running simulation.

> *Source: [You're Living Inside a Prediction](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4bdwDYd7rdE)*

### Core Mental Models

**Control Dichotomy**: You control exactly two things — your actions and your reactions. Everything else is noise. Find satisfaction in the process itself.

**Personal Fiction**: Memories are malleable Wikipedia pages, rewritten each time you recall them. Since you're already the editor, consciously choose which parts of your story to emphasize.

**The Happiness Trap**: Happiness is a byproduct of meaning, growth, and contribution — not a destination. Your brain's hedonic set point means external achievements only temporarily raise mood.

**The Effort Paradox**: The harder you desperately force an outcome (sleep, happiness, love), the more it eludes you. Practice effort without attachment.

**Cognitive Bias Awareness**: Your brain is a pattern-matching machine, not truth-seeking. It runs confirmation bias, illusory superiority, and others to edit reality. Awareness of these filters is your superpower.

### Intelligence — Cognitive Skills

**Logical Thinking**: Apply analysis, formalization, verification, and reasoning on rigorous logical basis. Foster fundamental and applied mathematics: discrete math, statistics, optimization.

**Critical Thinking**: Question deeply with "why, what-if-not, what-else" approach. Challenge assumptions. Refresh perspectives.

**Strategic Thinking**: Make balanced decisions with consideration for broader context and long-term outcomes.

**Problem Solving**: Diagnose issues with attention to detail. Identify root causes. Estimate required efforts.

**Creativity**: Embrace innovation, flexibility, and paradoxical thinking to break conventional patterns.

## Cognitive Biases and Logical Fallacies

### What is Cognitive Bias?

A **cognitive bias** is a systematic error in thinking
that occurs when people are processing and interpreting information, affecting the decisions and judgments they make.

Biases occur due to limitations of the human brain:

- Simplifying information processing via creating shortcuts
- Problems with memory, attention, attribution
- Ego motivations
- Social pressures, tribalism
- Attaching deeper identity/emotional meaning

### Categories of Cognitive Biases

#### Laziness of Mind

| Bias | Description |
| ------ | ------------- |
| **Mere Exposure** | Tendency to develop a preference for things simply because they are familiar. |
| **Confirmation Bias** | Tendency to seek out, interpret, and remember information that confirms existing beliefs while dismissing contradictory evidence. |
| **Anchoring** | Relying too heavily on the first piece of information (the "anchor"); subsequent judgments adjust from that anchor. |
| **Availability Heuristic** | Placing greater value on information that comes to mind quickly. |
| **Commitment and Consistency** | Tendency to behave consistently with commitments once made. |
| **Misinformation Effect** | Post-event information interfering with memory of the original event. |
| **Attentional Bias** | Paying attention to some things while simultaneously ignoring others. |
| **Functional Fixedness** | Seeing objects as only working in a particular way. |
| **Illusory Truth Effect** | Believing information if it's repeated often, regardless of accuracy. |
| **Cognitive Dissonance** | Feeling discomfort when holding conflicting beliefs, leading to changes in attitudes or behaviors. |
| **Halo Effect** | Overall impression of a person influences how you think about their character. |

#### Egoism

| Bias | Description |
| ------ | ------------- |
| **Self-Serving Bias** | Blaming external forces for failures and taking credit for successes. |
| **Actor-Observer Bias** | Attributing your own actions to external causes while attributing others' behaviors to internal causes. |
| **Dunning-Kruger Effect** | Less skilled individuals overestimate their abilities; experts tend to underestimate theirs. |
| **False Consensus Effect** | Overestimating how much other people agree with you. |
| **Optimism Bias** | Believing you are less likely to suffer misfortune and more likely to succeed than your peers. |

#### Tribalism

| Bias | Description |
| ------ | ------------- |
| **Social Proof** | Looking to others for cues on how to behave, especially in unfamiliar situations. |
| **Bandwagon Effect** | Adopting beliefs or behaviors because many others do the same. |
| **In-Group Favoritism** | Favoring members of one's own group over those in other groups. |
| **Reciprocity** | Wanting to give back when we receive something. |

#### Evaluation

| Bias | Description |
| ------ | ------------- |
| **Visual Shorthand** | Valuing things that look more complex or higher effort. |
| **Loss Aversion** | Preferring to avoid losses over acquiring equivalent gains. |
| **Sunk Cost Fallacy** | Continuing a behavior due to previously invested resources, even when irrational. |
| **Scarcity Bias** | Valuing things more when they are rare or perceived to be limited. |

### Logical Fallacies

| Fallacy | Description |
| --------- | ------------- |
| **Composition** | Assuming what is true of a part is also true of the whole. |
| **Division** | Assuming what is true of a whole must be true of all its parts. |
| **Circular Argument** | Using the same statement as both premise and conclusion. |
| **Non-Sequitur** | An argumentative leap where the conclusion does not follow from the premises. |
| **Irrelevant Conclusion** | Conclusion concerns something other than what the argument was trying to prove. |

### Communication Fallacies

| Fallacy | Description |
| --------- | ------------- |
| **Ambiguity** | A term has more than one meaning. |
| **Equivocation** | A single word used in two different senses. |
| **Amphiboly** | Ambiguity arising from sentence structure. |
| **Emphasis** | Incorrect emphasis of words in a sentence. |

### Contextual Fallacies

| Fallacy | Description |
| --------- | ------------- |
| **Quoting Out of Context** | Manipulation of the context of a quotation. |
| **Framing Effect** | How information is presented influences choices. |
| **Argument from Ignorance** | A statement cannot be proved true just because there is no evidence to disprove it. |
| **Ad Hominem** | Attacking the person rather than the argument. |
| **Appeal to Authority** | Truth cannot be proven merely because an "authority" says it. |
| **Appeal to Sentiments** | Using emotions to persuade rather than prove. |
| **Statistics Manipulation** | Statistics can be biased to "prove" many different hypotheses. |
