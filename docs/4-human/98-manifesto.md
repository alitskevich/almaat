# Manifesto of Humankind

![Manifesto of Humankind](/images/4-human/98-manifesto.svg)

Each Human is a manifestation of God — born equal in human dignity, which stems from our innate, limitless creative potential and the relentless striving to transcend beyond boundaries.

Our potential is expressed through curiosity, imagination, and thirst for beauty, and accumulates as cultural heritage, education, and science.

Each of us has the intrinsic right to pursue happiness — the feeling of realizing your potential in your own way, aligned with your abilities, visions, and aspirations.

## Rights

Rights are the conditions for the unfolding of [`Dignity`](21-human.md). They are natural, inalienable, and equal for all. They are not zero-sum. Rights are not given — they are taken, along with the obligation to defend them.

| Category | Domain |
| -------- | ------ |
| **Living** | Life, health, security, privacy, favorable conditions |
| **Liberty** | Identity, personal choice, self-determination |
| **Growth** | Education, access to knowledge, opportunity to develop |
| **Voice** | Open expression of views and ideas |
| **Partake** | Participation in social, cultural, and political life |

Safeguarding them requires:

- A safe, healthy, and prosperous life, equal protection under the law, and equitable access to food, housing, and economic opportunity — true equality needs more than formal rights.
- Freedom from violence, war, and coercion, paired with the duty of states and communities to resolve conflicts through dialogue and justice.
- Clean air, water, and a stable climate, balanced by the responsibility of environmental stewardship — to minimize ecological harm and leave future generations a healthy, sustainable world.
- Quality education, career development, and access to cultural heritage and public resources — with honest information in return, and the duty to seek truth and engage critically with media.
- Decent work conditions and healthcare: reasonable hours, paid leave, access to recreation and the arts. Flourishing includes mental health, rest, creativity, and play — not only work and productivity.
- Personal privacy in both the physical and digital spheres: data protection, control over one's image and information, freedom from unwarranted surveillance.
- Familial, communal, and associative bonds — caring relationships, mutual aid, and the freedom to form or join communities.

## Essential Freedoms

To realize this pursuit, each of us is entitled to:

- Freedom of thought, belief, and behavior.
- Freedom to define life's purpose and set personal goals.
- Freedom to choose and follow one's own path and methods.
- Freedom of expression and participation in civic life.

Freedom is the absence of external obstacles to exercising rights; it depends on [`Conscience`](21-human.md) spreading through culture and institutions. When rights or freedoms are absent or suppressed, the conscientious person works to restore them — for all, not only for themselves.

## Subsidiarity

Individual dignity is the measure of all laws and obligations. Power serves persons, not persons power.

## Transgression

Denying or suppressing dignity — of yourself or others. You cannot buy peace at the cost of your own dignity; past that line lies [`Kapot`](26-greatness.md).

## Reciprocal Respect

These freedoms and rights end where another's begin. Mutual recognition, respect, and protection of rights form the cornerstone of universal ethical, moral, and legal norms.

- **Individual responsibility.** We are all accountable for the conscious words and actions that violate mutual respect, and we owe one another empathy, compassion, and social cohesion.
- **Nobody as a means.** No one may be a means to any goal — only a final goal, by all means.
- **Non-discrimination.** Any attempt to curtail rights based on race, origin, group identity, or unfounded fears undermines human dignity and leads toward totalitarianism. Ends do not justify unjust means.
- **Accountability.** Governments, corporations, and institutions must be accountable to the people — transparent decision-making, anti-corruption measures, accessible channels for redress.
- **Minorities and identity.** Societies must protect minority rights and promote inclusive representation; each person may maintain and celebrate their cultural, linguistic, religious, and gender identity without fear of assimilation or erasure.

## Duty of Vigilance

It is the responsibility of every conscious individual to resist such injustices through critical thinking, education, public discourse, solidarity, and by creating spaces for growth and free expression.

- Non-violent resistance and conscientious objection are legitimate where states violate human dignity.
- Our shared humanity transcends borders: pandemics, climate change, and migration are collective responsibilities, requiring international collaboration and fair global institutions that respect sovereignty while ensuring basic rights everywhere.
- Net neutrality and equal access to digital technologies are enablers of participation.
- Social safety nets and fair wealth distribution prevent systemic poverty and marginalization.
