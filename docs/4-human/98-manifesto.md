# Manifesto of Humankind  

![Manifesto of Humankind](/images/3-socium/98-manifesto.svg)

Each Human is manifestation of God - born equal among others in your human dignity,
which is stemmed from our innate, limitless creative potential,
and the relentless striving to transcend beyond boundaries.

Our potential may expressed through our curiosity, imagination, and thirst for beauty, supported by self-belief, adaptability, perseverance, and the ability to collaborate, while accumulating cultural heritage, fostering education, and advancing science.

Each of us has the intrinsic right to pursue happiness,
which is feeling of realization of your potential in very own way, aligned with your abilities, visions, and aspirations.

## Rights

Rights are conditions for the unfolding of human dignity. They are natural, inalienable, and equal for all. They are not zero-sum. Rights are not given — they are taken, along with the obligation to defend them.

| Category | Domain |
| -------- | ------ |
| **Living** | Life, health, security, privacy, favorable conditions |
| **Liberty** | Identity, personal choice, self-determination |
| **Growth** | Education, access to knowledge, opportunity to develop |
| **Voice** | Open expression of views and ideas |
| **Partake** | Participation in social, cultural, and political life |

To safeguard these freedoms, we are deserve:

- A safe, healthy, and prosperous life
- Equal protection under the law
- An equal voice in society and public discourse
- Access to information, cultural heritage, and public resources
- Quality education and opportunities for career development
- Decent work conditions, environmental stewardship, and healthcare
- An individual’s right to live free from violence, war, and coercion—paired with the duty of states and communities to resolve conflicts through dialogue and justice.
- The right to clean air, water, and a stable climate, balanced by the responsibility to minimize ecological harm.
- Recognition that true equality requires not just formal rights but also equitable access to resources—food, housing, and economic opportunity.
- Acknowledgement that human flourishing includes mental health, rest, creativity, and play—not only work and productivity.
- The right to honest information and the duty of individuals to seek truth and engage critically with media.
- Support for reasonable working hours, paid leave, and access to recreation and the arts.
- The right to personal privacy in both physical and digital spheres, including data protection, control over one’s image and information, and freedom from unwarranted surveillance.
- The right to establish and sustain familial, communal, and associative bonds—caring relationships, mutual aid, and the freedom to form or join communities.

### Essential Freedoms

To realize this pursuit, each of us is entitled to:

- Freedom of thought, belief, and behavior
- Freedom to define life’s purpose and set personal goals
- Freedom to choose and follow own path and methods
- Freedom of expression and participation in civic life

Absence of external obstacles to exercising rights. Freedom depends on conscience spreading through culture and institutions.

When rights or freedoms are absent or suppressed, the conscientious person works to restore them — for all, not only for themselves.

### Subsidiarity

Individual dignity is the measure of all laws and obligations. Power serves persons, not persons power.

### Transgression

Denying or suppressing dignity — of yourself or others. You cannot buy peace at the cost of your own dignity.

#### Reciprocal Respect

These freedoms and rights end where another’s begin.
Mutual recognition, respect and protection of rights
form the cornerstone of universal ethical, moral, and legal norms.

- A duty to foster empathy, compassion, and social cohesion.
 Individual Responsibility We all are accountable for our conscious words and actions that violate this principle of mutual respect.
- Nobody could be a mean for any goals, but only final goal by all means.
 Non-Discrimination Any attempt to curtail rights based on race, origin, group identity, or unfounded fears undermines human dignity and leads toward totalitarianism. Ends do not justify unjust means.
- A principle that governments, corporations, and institutions must be accountable to the people—with transparent decision-making, anti-corruption measures, and accessible channels for redress.
- The duty of societies to protect minority rights and promote inclusive representation.
- The right to maintain and celebrate one’s cultural, linguistic, religious, and gender identity—without fear of assimilation or erasure.

#### Duty of Vigilance

It is the responsibility of every conscious individual to resist such injustices through critical thinking, education, public discourse, solidarity, and by creating spaces for growth and free expression.
  
- Recognition of non-violent resistance and conscientious objection where states violate human dignity.
- An affirmation that our shared humanity transcends borders—that we have collective responsibilities (pandemics, climate change, migration) that require international collaboration.
- Support for fair global institutions and policies that respect sovereignty while ensuring basic rights everywhere.
- Commitment to net neutrality and equal access to digital technologies as enablers of participation.
- A commitment to protect our planet and its ecosystems, ensuring that future generations inherit a healthy, sustainable world.
- Support for social safety nets and fair wealth distribution to prevent systemic poverty and marginalization.
