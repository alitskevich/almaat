# Attention

![Attention](/images/7-mastery/07-attention.svg)

What the working day is made of: attention profiles, the order of stimulation, and the block that must be protected to reach the work.

## Attention Profile

Attention is not uniform across people. The difference is a trait to be matched, not a defect to be corrected — and a system built for one profile fails silently in the hands of the other. The failure then reads as a personal flaw, which it is not.

| Profile | Strength | Cost |
| ------- | -------- | ---- |
| **Systematizing** | Holds a single track for long stretches; builds and maintains process; predictable output | Slow to switch; weak at originating from nothing |
| **Scanning** | Fast switching, wide search, sharp output under novelty; deep absorption in whatever engages | Poor at sustaining a system; drowns once many threads are open; the profile a [feed](../4-human/28-animal.md#reward-and-compulsion) is built to catch |

Neither profile is the productive one. Systematizing attention runs a process; scanning attention finds what the process should be. Most published productivity method is written by the first profile for the first profile, and reaches the second as evidence of being broken. A remedy whose first step already demands the capacity it is meant to build — read this book, maintain this system — cannot bootstrap; the entry step has to work at current capacity.

- **Match the role, not the person.** A scanning profile placed on process maintenance fails; a systematizing profile asked to originate fails. Move the work, not the character.
- **Delegate the mismatch instead of fixing it.** Second-brain tooling — layered note systems, tag graphs, multi-app stacks — is built by and for systematizing attention, and earns its cost in an organization large enough to staff someone to run it. Adopted alone as self-improvement, it becomes one more abandoned system.
- **Prefer a single, dumb capture surface.** One paper notebook outperforms four synced apps for a scanning profile: writing by hand encodes as it captures (see [Learning → Making It Stick](09-learning.md#making-it-stick)), and there is no structure to maintain, migrate, or search.
- **Arrangement first, chemistry second.** Medication addresses a deficit; it does not compensate for a day arranged against you. Stimulants do work, and they also raise the cost of every day taken without them. The arrangement below comes first either way.

## Focus and Priority

Focus on the most important task — the one that creates the most impact. Say no to everything else.

- **80/20 Rule**: 20% of activities create 80% of results. The rest is expensive noise.
- **Eat the frog first** — pick the most important, hardest action each day, and start it inside the [waking window](#the-shape-of-the-day), before the part of you that refuses is awake.
- **Cap what is open** — three active priorities, no more. An open thread costs attention whether or not it is being worked, so the fourth priority is paid for by the first three.
- **Dopamine detox** — eliminate shallow reward sources that compete with deep work. Order matters as much as removal: see [The Shape of the Day](#the-shape-of-the-day).

For the written form of this triage, see [Brian Tracy's method](03-vision.md#questions-on-paper).

## The Shape of the Day

The order of stimulation across a day decides what work is possible inside it. Two mechanisms set that order.

**Reward runs one way.** Waking baseline is low. Ordinary work sits low on the reward scale; a feed, a game, sugar, or an unread thread sits far above it. Once the day has touched the high end, the low end stops registering as worth doing — and intent does not bring it back, because the ratchet only turns up. So hold the entire working day below the level the work itself requires, and take the peaks after it: not before, not in the middle. One glance at a phone over breakfast is enough to spend the day.

**Resistance boots later than you do.** Sleep inertia is real: for the first minute or two after waking, the deliberative system that negotiates, postpones, and objects is not yet online. Begin the day's hardest task inside that window and the negotiation never takes place. This is the mechanism under [eat the frog](#focus-and-priority) — the frog is eaten before the part of you that refuses wakes up.

**Routines enter focus; waking already is focus.** Coffee, a cold shower, breathing, journaling, and affirmations are tools for *re-entering* focus later in the day, once it has been lost. Applied at wake-up they spend the one window that needed no tool. The same holds for supplements and biohacking: they scale an arrangement that works, and cannot replace one that doesn't.

| Move | Why |
| ---- | --- |
| **Decide the target while rested** | Choose tomorrow's first task during today's working hours. An evening decision is made by a depleted `Agent`, and planning at night restarts the mind against sleep. Plan the whole week in one rested sitting. |
| **Leave the work open** | Laptop open on the right file, everything else closed; notebook and headphones set out. Waking should require no decision — only sitting down. |
| **Phone out of the room overnight** | Not silenced — absent. It is picked up after the first block, never before it. |
| **Work first, eat second** | Take the first block before breakfast, then eat, then continue. See [Vitality → Nutrition](../8-sustain/01-vitality.md#6-nutrition). |
| **Hard-stop the day** | Fix the working day at a length you can actually hold — six hours is a common ceiling — and stop there. Work expands into whatever it is given; a hard edge forces the compression and exposes what should be delegated. |
| **Peaks after the stop** | Games, feeds, sugar, loud music — once the day is closed, and not before. |

## Protecting the Block

Entering focus starts with a struggle phase; a block takes roughly twenty minutes to become productive, and any interruption costs those twenty minutes again. A day spent inside notifications is therefore a day that never reaches the productive part at all.

- **Notification rights are granted by name, not held by default.** Two or three people may reach you mid-block. Everyone else waits, and learns to route through them.
- **Give the sender that matters its own sound.** When every message sounds alike, every sound is an unanswered question. One distinct tone for the one person who cannot wait makes every other sound safely ignorable.
- **Use one track as a cue.** The same music every block, off the moment the block ends, becomes a conditioned signal — the sound comes to mean work. Avoid high-arousal or melancholy music while working: arousal you do not discharge is a cost, not a help. Keep the cue bounded — continuous audio is itself input, and a mind never left quiet stops returning to low-stimulus work.
