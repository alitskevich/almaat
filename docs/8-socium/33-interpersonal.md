# Influence

## Two methods of social improvement

1. **External**: constrain evil, compel good
2. **Ethical**: work on yourself — provide example rather than correcting others

The second method is primary.

## Interpersonal Mastery

The only thing that truly matters is having people you love and who love you.

**Reliability**: Honor commitments consistently. Reputation is your most important asset — it takes decades to build and a moment to destroy.

**Emotional Intelligence**: Read social dynamics. Listen actively. Support others with genuine empathy.

**Communication**: Find common language. Navigate opposing viewpoints. Share your vision clearly.

**Collaboration**: Accept criticism. Resolve conflicts. Foster a safe environment.

**Transcend**: Prioritize shared goals over personal ones. Confidence without humility is arrogance; the combination makes someone worth following.

## Charisma

The distinctive uniqueness and attractiveness of an integrated person.

The Whole Person arises through conscious recognition and acceptance of one's shadow, making peace with it.

- It is the source of authenticity, creativity, and emotional depth.
- It evokes interest, respect, and inspiration in others — but also fear.

Your body sends "avoid me" signals before you say a word, and the decisive first impression forms in just 7 seconds. Charisma isn't an innate gift but a trainable skill built on the formula warmth + power + presence.

Eye contact: hold someone's gaze for 60–70% of the conversation (100% feels like an interrogation, 30% reads as avoidance). The trick: focus on the color of the person's eyes to naturally hold the right duration.

Pace and pauses: slow your speech by about 20%, pause for roughly 1.5 seconds before answering, and cut filler words ("um," "like," "sort of") that just fill the silence you're afraid of.

Posture and body: open shoulders, visible palms (an ancient "I'm safe" signal), chin parallel to the floor, take up space, and walk confidently with a long stride.

Warmth beats intelligence: the brain first checks "friend or foe," and only then "smart or not." Warmth is switched on by a genuine Duchenne smile (with the eyes), using the person's name twice in a conversation, and listening to understand rather than to reply. This echoes the emphasis in Glasp sources that active listening is the key to genuine connection and making the other person feel valued.
Low voice: breathe from the belly, end your phrases with a falling intonation (a period, not a question), keep volume around 70%, and record yourself to correct your delivery.

Specifics over abstractions: details, numbers, names, and smells create a mental "picture," and stories (the formula situation → twist → lesson) are remembered about 22 times better than facts.
Mirroring with a 2–3 second delay (not mimicry), but never mirror aggression; leading mirroring gently pulls the other person into your calm state.

Use fewer words: one thought, period, with no unnecessary apologies or justifications for "existing" ("glad we talked" instead of "thanks for hearing me out").

The strongest and most dangerous technique — high status + vulnerability: only after mastering the first eight, add one small, sincere admission of weakness (the "Pratfall effect"). The conclusion: charisma is intention + repetition; add one technique per day over 9 days.

#### Core Sequence

1. **Align to your audience** — Know their psychology, needs, and motivations. Start from their point of view. Show how they benefit.
2. **Build credibility first** — Establish authority before attempting influence. Never threaten their identity or predictability.
3. **Appeal to emotion and logic** — Both systems drive decisions.
4. **Use clear, concise language** — Complexity creates resistance.
5. **Provide evidence** — Ground claims in concrete reality.
6. **Address counterarguments** — Preemptive neutralization strengthens your position.
7. **Frame effectively** — Context determines meaning.
8. **Create urgency** — Motivate action through relevance and time pressure.
9. **Repeat** — Familiarity breeds acceptance.
10. **End with a clear call to action** — Direct behavior explicitly.

### Interpersonal Dynamics

**Respect dignity equally; differentiate by behavior and merit.**
People are equal in inherent dignity. People differ in mindset, conduct, and competence. Don't measure others by yourself.

**Choose your circle.**
Do not work with toxic people regardless of talent. The people around you shape you. If you're the smartest in the room, find a new room.

**Think positive-sum.**
The best way to win is to help the other person win.

**Ask for what you want.**
You'll get yes more often than you expect. Ask for advice rather than feedback — people are flattered and give more useful answers.

**Nobody is watching you as much as you think.**
Everyone is focused on themselves.

### Elicitation

In 5 minutes of casual conversation you can learn someone's family dynamics, financial situation, personality type, and emotional patterns — without asking directly.

**Method:**

1. Ask questions, don't tell stories
2. Validate their feelings: "I agree, I feel the same way"
3. Collect information through open and closed questions
4. Only share reflections — mirror what they've already told you
5. The structure stays invisible because the flow feels natural

**Emotional mirroring creates trust.**
When you reflect someone's emotional state, they assume shared experience — which translates to trust.

**Body language — eye direction (left-to-right readers):**

- Looking left → accessing memory, truth, past events
- Looking right → creating, imagining, fabricating

Establish a baseline first: ask known-truth questions to see their pattern before drawing conclusions.

**Assess emotional state before engaging.**
Use body language and tone to understand where they are. Connect to whatever state they're in.

### Agency

**Take ownership.**
Stop waiting for permission. You are the only one who can make it happen.

**Take responsibility** for things in your sphere, even if not your fault. It builds confidence and control.

**Ask more, talk less.**
Real listening builds connection and insight faster than eager answering.

**Maintain connections.**
When in doubt, reach out. Push past the awkwardness.

**Say yes early, no later.**
Youth favors yes. Experience favors no. Say yes to opportunities; learn to say no to distractions.

**Send thank-you notes.**
One handwritten note every two weeks. Gratitude compounds.
